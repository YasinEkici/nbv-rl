### D-016
Method: parts are sorted by the hard-to-see ratio from the Slice 1 candidate screening and cut into 3 near-equal strata (5 / 5 / 4 parts). From each stratum one part is drawn at random (seed 0) for validation and one for test; the rest go to train. Validation and test therefore each contain one easy, one medium, and one hard part. Implemented in `nbv.parts.stratified_split`; the result is in configs/split_v1.yaml.
Result: val = 271875, 591247, 37275; test = 178195, 486859, 79729; train = the other 8.
Why: 2 test geometries (D-013) are too few for a generalization claim. Scan-RL (Peralta et al. 2020, arXiv 2008.12664) uses Houses3K batches of 50 geometries × 5 textures with a 90% / 10% geometry split, i.e. 5 test geometries per batch; with 14 parts we only partly close this gap.

### D-020
Updated by D-047 (2026-11-05): passes are now computed per base part from its nominal mesh and shared by variants (D-029); the per-variant wording below is historical.
Why the standoff rule: in laser-scanner planning the dominant constraints are the field of view, the depth of field, and the maximum view angle (Zussman, Schuler & Seliger 1994, Int J Adv Manuf Technol 9:56–64). With a fixed 200 mm sphere around the center, a 200 mm part would reach within ~27 mm of the sensor and most front surfaces would sit closer than the 150 mm near limit of the depth of field. With R(u) = h(u) + (200 − 50) mm the near limit touches the part's outermost point in direction u, and the sensor origin stays ≥ 150 mm from the part.
Limitation: from a given direction, surface more than 100 mm (the depth of field) behind the part's outermost point cannot be measured.
The sweep direction x − (x·d)d has norm ≥ sin(10°) on the cap, so it is defined everywhere, including the zenith. Directions are part-independent; standoffs and sweep lengths are computed per variant.

### D-021
Updated by D-047 (2026-11-05): since ENV v4, static robot–part and robot–table collision checks are included at the five sweep samples (D-042). Self-collision and transfers remain unchecked.
- Model: `rtb.models.DH.UR5` (the URDF model clones example-robot-data from GitHub at first use). Joint limits ±360° per the UR5 datasheet; the DH model ships ±180°, which made wrist joints jump by ~2π between sweep samples.
- IK: `ik_LM` on the DH model does not apply `robot.tool` consistently, so IK is solved for the flange pose in the base frame on a bare model (tol 1e-10, ~1e-5 m worst pose error). `ik_LM`'s own restarts (`slimit` > 1) use an unseeded RNG, so each start is a separate call with `slimit = 1`: q_home first, then 8 starts drawn with seed 0; the solution closest to q_home is kept. Later samples are seeded with the previous solution.
- Wrap guard (added 2026-10-30): a joint whose sweep midpoint is more than 270° from q_home is shifted by a multiple of 2π (whole sweep together, within limits, closest to q_home); other joints are left as solved. Two stricter rules were tried and rejected because they split passes near ±π on joint 6 and inflated PTP moves: wrapping every joint by the start sample (moves > 270°: 48 → 6468 of 88322 at 48 candidates) and by the midpoint to within π (744 → 1829 of 626012 at 128 candidates).
- Table clearance is checked on DH frame origins and the sensor origin only; robot–part and self-collision are not modeled (size measured by the diagnostic of D-042).
- Masking limitation (added 2026-11-03): reachability is checked at 5 discrete points along each sweep; discrete checks can miss problems between the samples, the known limit of sampling-based collision checking (LARC, arXiv 2608.29767). The |Δq| ≤ 45° rule between samples reduces this risk but does not remove it. Removing unreachable views by IK and collision checks is standard practice in view-planning benchmarks (ObjView-Bench).
- DH parameter note (added 2026-11-03): the toolbox's DH UR5 uses d1 = 0.089459 m, the UR datasheet 0.089159 m. The difference moves the shoulder by 0.3 mm; an independent forward-kinematics check (sanity check B3) agrees with the toolbox to 0.011 mm when it uses the toolbox value. The toolbox value is kept.
- Base position superseded by D-027 (−350 mm).
- Finding (at the original base position): with the base at (−400, 0, 0) mm, robot-side views whose flange falls within ~100 mm of the base axis are kinematically unreachable (0 of 200 random IK starts succeed); far-side low views are out of reach. A base at −500 … −650 mm recovers robot-side views but loses the far side (diagnostic on one part during Slice 2, computed before the IK tool-frame fix, so only qualitative).

### D-022
Source: Universal Robots, URScript Programming Language manual, `movej` (default joint speed 1.05 rad/s, acceleration 1.4 rad/s²). For the leading joint displacement Δq: t = Δq/v + v/a if Δq ≥ v²/a, else t = 2·sqrt(Δq/a). Reference values (UR5 datasheet): all joints up to 180°/s, reach 850 mm. PTP matrix entry (i, j) = end of pass i → start of pass j; diagonal 0; inf for unreachable passes. Times from/to q_home are stored separately for episode start and end.

### D-024
- The environment (Plan 001 Slice 4) tracks the arm's actual joint configuration. A move to pass j uses, per joint, the [−π, π] difference to pass j's start, unless the whole sweep of pass j would then leave the ±360° limits, in which case the next valid 2π shift is used; the arm's state after the pass is the shifted end configuration.
- Cached per base part (data/cache/robot/nominal/): `ptp_pi`, `from_home_pi`, `to_home_pi` from [−π, π]-wrapped differences (a lower bound that ignores limits). Planners (greedy, TSP) use them; their chosen pass sequences and the TSP tour are re-evaluated in the environment before any number is reported. The raw-difference matrices `ptp`, `from_home`, `to_home` were cached until 2026-11-03 but never read; they were removed from the code and the cache files.

### D-025
Assumptions: the camera's field of view covers the laser line over the whole depth of field; the camera sits on the +s side (leading in the sweep direction) at b = 200 · tan 25° ≈ 93.3 mm and aims at the working-distance point on the laser axis; ray origins are pushed 0.05 mm along the surface normal; the table is a plane at the variant's min z − 0.5 mm. Samples are drawn on the subdivided base mesh as (face, barycentric) with seed [0, file_id] and evaluated on each variant (same topology); normals come from the variant's faces. Line-of-sight rays are cast only where the geometric test and the matching angle test pass. Layers (geom, laser angle, laser LOS, per-camera angle and LOS) are stored bit-packed per variant in data/cache/visibility/, with a camera axis so a second camera (dual-camera ablation) needs no layout change. Unit tests use a 60 mm cube because the fan is only 75 mm wide at the 150 mm near limit.
Finding (Slice 3; numbers recomputed on 2026-11-03 on the train + val parts only): the union over all passes covers 40.6% (48 candidates) / 44.2% (128) of the samples (mean over the 55 train + val variants). Causes: samples facing down onto the table (normal within 10° of −z: 2–20% per part), deep interior surface (a ray along the outward normal hits the part again for 22–57% of the samples per part), and the angle and dual-occlusion tests. This follows from our selection criteria (internal cavities, ribs, deep holes), which make the parts occlusion-heavy on purpose; it is not a general property of Thingi10K. The literature calls this the omnidirectional self-occlusion ceiling (ObjView-Bench, Pan et al. 2026, arXiv 2605.10707). Coverage targets are relative to the reachable union (D-023), so 95% stays attainable.
The ~40% ceiling (added 2026-11-03): interior surfaces cannot be measured from outside by any optical sensor. Industry uses computed tomography (CT) for them; CT is the only tool for inaccessible interior regions but is expensive and slow (Carmignato 2012; SAE 2006-01-0510 reports 4–15 h for automated CT). Normalizing coverage by the measurable surface is therefore physically right, not a convenience. Flipping the part for a second setup (common in industry, e.g. the Laser Design flip-plate fixture) would open the table-facing samples; Plan 003 → Open issues.
Ideal-sensor assumptions (added 2026-11-03): the analytic sweep itself neglects only the discrete profile spacing (0.1 mm at 100 mm/s and 1 kHz; negligible). The real limitations come from the ideal sensor model: measurement is binary, whereas precision degrades gradually with the incidence angle (first order ~1/cos α); multiple reflections on shiny surfaces are not modeled; the lower accuracy at the ends of the laser line is not modeled; there is no measurement noise; the camera's field of view is assumed to cover the whole line. Listed in PROTOCOL → Model limitations; quality-weighted coverage is future work (Plan 001 → Open issues).

### D-029

### D-030
- α = 50 s per unit coverage puts ΔC = 0.1 at about one step's time (median step 4.7 s on train parts); with Φ = 0 at the end, α does not change the optimal policy. B = 250 s exceeds K_max × the longest step + return home (~175 s at 48, ~230 s at 128 candidates), so every success beats every failure. K_max ≈ 1.5 × the most passes a greedy needed on train parts (16 at 48, 23 at 128). All from train parts only (Slice 3 diagnostic).
- Potential-based shaping (Ng, Harada & Russell 1999): with discount 1 and Φ = α·C, α·ΔC = Φ(s') − Φ(s). For policy invariance in episodic tasks, Φ must be 0 at terminal states (Grześ 2017); otherwise α·C_end is added to the return and over-shooting 95% and partial coverage at K_max are rewarded. Option (i), Φ = 0 at the end, makes the return equal to −cycle time + B·success, the same objective as the primary metric; option (ii), keeping Φ, is a flag (`terminal_potential_zero: false`) for an ablation. With discount γ < 1 (Phase 2) the term is α·(γ·C' − C); the reward takes the discount as a parameter. Precedent for the structure: Scan-RL uses coverage gain, a distance penalty, a step penalty and a terminal bonus (Peralta et al. 2020).
- Invalid actions are masked, not penalized (Huang & Ontañón 2022); a masked action raises an error.
- The step count is part of the state, so ending at K_max is an end of the task and Φ = 0 applies there too; the Gymnasium flag was `truncated` so that wrappers handle it as a budget end.
- Changed by D-038 (2026-11-03): reaching K_max returns terminated = True, truncated = False. The step count is in the observation, so the budget is part of the task; a true end is consistent with the definition of the return (−cycle time + B·success, nothing after K_max), and it avoids the bias that a finite horizon creates for potential-based shaping when the last state is bootstrapped (Canonaco et al. 2024). With `truncated`, SB3 bootstraps the value of the last state (stable_baselines3/common/on_policy_algorithm.py, TimeLimit handling), which would treat the budget end as a time limit. The reward weights α, discount and the terminal-potential option moved to the shaping wrapper (D-038); β = γ_t = 1 and reward_scale were dropped, since the objective is the cycle time itself.
- Random part pose: interface only (`pose_id`, default 0); precomputing 8 yaw angles would take ~25 min and ~1 GB for all variants, deferred to Plan 003.

### D-033
- Positioning: set-cover ILP + TSP is the closest counterpart of automated CAD-based planning in industry (e.g. the virtual measuring room of ZEISS ScanBox / ScanCobot, where sensor positions and robot paths are computed from the CAD and the measurement plan); we call it the industry-type CAD-based planner. It is a two-stage decomposition (fewest passes, then shortest tour), not the optimum of the joint objective.
- Fixed pattern: not "current industrial practice", but the robot analogue of stepping a turntable in front of a fixed scanner (in the literature the number of steps and the height of that scheme are optimized with ray-traced virtual scans; Kot et al.). Definition: one pass nearest to the zenith plus a ring of 8 directions at 45° elevation (azimuth 0°, 45°, …), each snapped to the nearest reachable candidate of the nominal model; duplicates skipped; order top, then ring by azimuth; executed open loop with repair. Success without repair is reported separately.
- Information: greedy-IG maximizes predicted new samples (nominal visibility); greedy-IG/cost divides by the move estimate (ptp-style [−π, π] movej from the current joint state) + scan time; greedy-oracle maximizes new samples of the real variant. MCTS (UCT, 200 simulations per real step, expansion width 8 by the cost-aware score, ε = 0.2 greedy rollouts, cost = ptp_pi + scan + return home, failure cost 250 s) plans on the nominal model from the measured samples; precedent: non-myopic MCTS view planning with robot arms (Best et al. 2019). If the nominal model already predicts the target while real coverage is below it, MCTS takes the cost-aware greedy pass (same rule as repair).
- Random + TSP: K = ⌈1.5 · d_plan95⌉ of the part (from the nominal CAD), ~1.6 × the mean planning difficulty of the main set, the budget at which this baseline is competitive in ObjView-Bench.
- Solvers: CP-SAT (8 workers, seed 0, 60 s per stage; status and gap recorded) for set cover and max coverage. Set cover is solved in two stages (fewest passes, then a seeded distinct-weight tie-break with the count fixed), so the cover is optimal and reproducible. Changed on 2026-10-31: a single-stage solve with 8 workers returned different optimal covers on repeated runs (ILP + TSP not reproducible); 1 worker did not reach the optimal 95% cover at 128 candidates within 60 s (e.g. 20 instead of 17 passes). Equally minimal covers can differ a lot in tour time, so ILP + TSP depends on this tie-break; the joint optimum (D-035) removes that arbitrariness. Registry rows from both earlier settings are superseded; sample classes with identical coverage columns are merged before solving. TSP on ptp_pi with home as start and end: exact Held-Karp up to 16 passes, otherwise OR-Tools routing (guided local search, 10 s). Every plan is re-evaluated in the environment (D-024).
- Consistency test: greedy maximum coverage only guarantees a 1 − 1/e approximation, not the optimum (Nemhauser, Wolsey & Fisher 1978), and this bound is tight (Feige 1998). So greedy-oracle ≥ greedy-IG holds only at k = 1. For k > 1 the test uses the exact k-pass max-coverage ILP on the real variant as an upper bound of every method (tests/test_baselines.py: k ≤ 14 on 2 train + 2 val variants, k ≤ 4 on a synthetic part; until 2026-11-03 also scripts/ilp_bound.py → results/ilp_bound_v1.csv, removed because its ILP + TSP column came from the irreproducible single-stage set cover).
- d_plan / d_plan95: fewest passes covering 100% / 95% of the nominal reachable union (set-cover ILP), per base part, for 48 and 128 candidates (results/d_plan_v2.csv, ENV v4; results/d_plan_v1.csv is historical, v1–v3 masks). It uses only the CAD, so it is computed for test parts too; test values are never used in any tuning decision.

### D-038
- Updated by D-048 (2026-11-05): in Plan 002's feature space, linear Φ = 50*C = 50*bias − 50*remaining exactly, with C from shaping.measured_share and no clipping. With reward_scale=0.01 the corresponding initial weight shift is +0.5 on bias, −0.5 on remaining. Wiewiora's equivalence extends to SARSA/TD: zero-initialized linear shaping equals no shaping with that initialization. The true online SARSA test follows identical seeded actions for at least 3000 steps for each λ in {0.5, 0.9, 1}, with a constant weight difference to 1e-9. Terminal features are zero for bootstrapping (terminal Φ=0). Convex and value_estimate are not generally represented by this state-only linear combination and remain different shaping choices.
- (a) Why the split: for any potential Φ with Φ = 0 at terminal states, the optimal policy is unchanged (Ng, Harada & Russell 1999; terminal Φ = 0 in episodic tasks, Grześ 2017). Policy invariance does not guarantee faster learning: the choice, scale and shift of Φ decide whether shaping helps (Müller & Kudenko, AAMAS 2025); a potential close to the optimal value function is recommended (Canonaco et al. 2024, arXiv 2404.07826); PBRS gives only a marginal gain in convergence speed but is robust to scaling (Jeon et al. 2023). The environment therefore fixes only the objective (cycle time, success), and Φ is an agent setting. The convex option rewards a coverage gain more as coverage rises, similar to DA-NBV (arXiv 2608.08025). For tabular learners PBRS is equivalent to initializing Q with Φ (Wiewiora 2003); for the Phase 1 linear agents, Φ thus acts mostly as an initial value estimate.
- Φ options (`nbv.shaping`): `none`; `linear` = α·C (the v2 shaping); `convex` = α·C² (exponent parameter); `value_estimate` = −T, T = remaining time estimated by greedy-IG/cost on the nominal model (moves as [−π, π] movej estimates, then home). C is the measured share of the nominal reachable union, because the real reachable union is privileged; it differs from the real coverage by at most the nominal/real union mismatch. α = 50 s and the discount are wrapper parameters (defaults from D-030). Shaping terms of an episode sum to −Φ(s_0) with discount 1 (0 for linear and convex); tests in tests/test_shaping.py, including that Φ never reads the real part.
- (c) info["agent"]: pass, times, joint motion, reward parts, nominal coverage, cycle time, success (visible to the agent through the bonus anyway), action mask; info["eval"]: real primary, NSC and raw coverage, coverage gain, nominal − real gap. tests/test_privileged.py checks statically (AST search over nbv.features, nbv.baselines, nbv.shaping, nbv.agents) and at run time (real part replaced by an object that fails on access) that agent-side code reads neither; `greedy_oracle_policy` is the only allowed exception.
- Baselines do not read the reward, so their metrics do not change; checked on 2026-11-03 by rerunning the 144 active exploratory v2 runs with v3 (random + TSP: seed 0 only, 108 runs, 2 970 episodes, train + val, 48 and 128 candidates): pass sequences, repair flags, cycle times, every metric of `nbv.evaluation.episode_metrics` and the return are identical in every episode. With discount 1, β = γ_t = 1 and Φ = 0 at the end, the v2 return was already −cycle time + B·success, so even the returns are equal.
- The 144 active exploratory v2 registry rows are superseded (older ENV_VERSION).

### D-042
- Geometry (assumptions, not product data): the DH model puts the arm on the joint-2/3/4 axis line; the real UR5 has its upper arm 135.85 mm and its forearm 16.15 mm off that line (shoulder +135.85, elbow −119.7, wrist 1 +93.0, summing to DH d4 = 109.15 mm), so the capsules carry these offsets.
